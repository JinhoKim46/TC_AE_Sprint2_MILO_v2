# C02 — Dispatch disruption and access constraints

> SYNTHETIC CASE. Author-generated, not domain-validated. No real customer data, verified savings or deployed results. Narrative adapted from supplied story 2; interview answers and evidence are invented teaching fixtures.

## Business and opening narrative

22 staff; 15 field technicians covering a city and surrounding towns.

“Our dispatcher plans tomorrow in a spreadsheet, but urgent morning calls dismantle the route. The nearest worker may lack the skill, part or customer authorization. We redirected someone to a school whose key holder was absent, losing both the urgent visit and the original slot.”

## Current workflow and failure boundary

1. Dispatcher plans route
2. Urgent request arrives
3. Dispatcher calls technicians
4. Appointment changes
5. Technician waits or works
6. Times recorded inconsistently

The critical failure is: Replanning occurs without explicit feasibility constraints or acknowledged commitments.

## Intervention comparison

1. **Process first:** Collect impact, access window and required skills before replanning; freeze a near-term travel window unless the dispatcher explicitly overrides it.
2. **Conventional software and bounded AI assistance:** Rank feasible assignments; display travel, skills, stock and access tradeoffs; issue versioned schedule changes requiring acknowledgement.
3. **Reject as the default:** wholesale platform replacement or autonomous agents without evidence that a simpler process/configuration change is inadequate.

AI may extract drafts, retrieve candidate cases and explain differences. Permissions, approvals, counters, calculations, state transitions and audit history belong in deterministic application code.
