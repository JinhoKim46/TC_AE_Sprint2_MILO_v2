# C15 — Branch access and head-office approval conflict

> SYNTHETIC CASE. Author-generated, not domain-validated. No real customer data, verified savings or deployed results. Narrative adapted from supplied story 15; interview answers and evidence are invented teaching fixtures.

## Business and opening narrative

Maintenance provider for multi-site retail and education groups; one customer has 28 branches.

“Head office pays and approves parts, but branches call and control access. Branches expect appointments before approval arrives. Equipment moves between similar-named locations. Several apparently separate failures turned out to follow one central configuration change.”

## Current workflow and failure boundary

1. Branch reports
2. Site and asset identified
3. Diagnosis
4. Head-office approval sought
5. Branch appointment arranged
6. Work reported centrally and locally

The critical failure is: Customer hierarchy, device location and approval dependencies are not modelled consistently.

**Alternative / non-applicability:** Similar symptoms across branches may be independent hardware faults; common timing is a hypothesis, not proof.

For a new customer, request records corresponding to the evidence ledger. A matching story is a retrieval cue, not proof of the cause. Resolve contradictory dates, identities and authority before selecting an intervention.

## Intervention comparison

1. **Process first:** Establish organization/site/device identities and separate access approval from spending approval; retain local child tickets under confirmed shared incidents.
2. **Conventional software and bounded AI assistance:** Link related incidents for review; route approvals to scoped authorities; provide role-filtered views and readiness checks.
3. **Reject as the default:** wholesale platform replacement or autonomous agents without evidence that a simpler process/configuration change is inadequate.

AI may extract drafts, retrieve candidate cases and explain differences. Permissions, approvals, counters, calculations, state transitions and audit history belong in deterministic application code.
