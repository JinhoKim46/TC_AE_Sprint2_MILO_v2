# C11 — Contract revenue hides service burden

> SYNTHETIC CASE. Author-generated, not domain-validated. No real customer data, verified savings or deployed results. Narrative adapted from supplied story 11; interview answers and evidence are invented teaching fixtures.

## Business and opening narrative

20 staff; approximately 100 recurring contracts.

“We know revenue and payroll but not service cost by account. Short remote calls look like long on-site repairs when both count as one ticket. Driving, repeat visits and van parts are missing. Renewal prices follow intuition.”

## Current workflow and failure boundary

1. Serve customer
2. Record main task
3. Buy parts centrally
4. Receive recurring fee
5. Review revenue
6. Negotiate renewal

The critical failure is: Inconsistent activity and cost attribution prevents meaningful contract-level economics.

## Intervention comparison

1. **Process first:** Collect lightweight activity categories and parts attribution; disclose completeness; agree direct cost and overhead conventions separately.
2. **Conventional software and bounded AI assistance:** Aggregate attributable time and material cost; show ranges or unknowns for missing inputs and flag expensive repeat patterns.
3. **Reject as the default:** wholesale platform replacement or autonomous agents without evidence that a simpler process/configuration change is inadequate.

AI may extract drafts, retrieve candidate cases and explain differences. Permissions, approvals, counters, calculations, state transitions and audit history belong in deterministic application code.
