# Technician tiers

## Why the team size changes the price

Most of the work the modules do grows with the field team. More technicians mean more visits to plan, more vans holding stock, more completion reports and more open jobs to follow. The rate card therefore scales the base monthly price of every chosen module by a technician factor that depends on how many field technicians the business has.

## The tiers

| Field technicians | Technician factor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 5 | 1.00 |
| 6 to 15 | 1.30 |
| 16 to 30 | 1.60 |
| more than 30 | priced by a person |

A business with up to 5 field technicians pays the base prices unchanged, with a factor of 1.00. A business with 6 to 15 field technicians has its module prices multiplied by 1.30. A business with 16 to 30 field technicians has its module prices multiplied by 1.60. The factor applies to the module prices only; integration surcharges and the setup fee are not multiplied.

As an example, a business with nine field technicians that chooses a module with a base price of 400 USD would see 520 USD a month for that module before surcharges, because nine falls in the 1.30 tier.

## Above 30 technicians

Above 30 technicians a person prices the work. The rate card stops at 30 because larger teams usually have several dispatchers, regional depots or their own development staff, and a fixed factor would no longer describe the effort. When the recorded count is above 30, the estimate tool does not return a number, and the assistant explains that someone will prepare the offer.

## What counts as a technician

The technician count is the number of field technicians, not all staff. Count the people who travel to customer sites or work on customer devices, including those who split their time between the workshop and the road. Do not count office staff, dispatchers, accounts staff, sales people or the owner, unless the owner also goes out on jobs as a regular part of the week.

Subcontractors who work for the business regularly are a judgment call; the assistant asks the owner whether they should be counted and records the answer as the owner gave it. If the owner gives a range, such as "eight or nine", the assistant asks which number to use rather than picking one.
