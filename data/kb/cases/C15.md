# C15: Branch access and head-office approval conflict

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

Maintenance provider for multi-site retail and education groups; one customer has 28 branches.

“Head office pays and approves parts, but branches call and control access. Branches expect appointments before approval arrives. Equipment moves between similar-named locations. Several apparently separate failures turned out to follow one central configuration change.”

**Typical symptom:** Head office pays and approves parts, but branches call and control access. Several apparently separate failures turned out to follow one central configuration change.

## Current workflow

1. Branch reports
2. Site and asset identified
3. Diagnosis
4. Head-office approval sought
5. Branch appointment arranged
6. Work reported centrally and locally

## Diagnostic questions

### C15-Q01: Who approves expenditure and access?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Head office approves cost; the branch agrees access.

**Why ask:** These are separate permissions.

**If the answer differs:** Similar symptoms across branches may be independent hardware faults; common timing is a hypothesis, not proof.

### C15-Q02: Are assets tracked after moves?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** The spreadsheet often keeps the old branch.

**Why ask:** Location needs effective dates.

### C15-Q03: Did failures start together?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Three branches failed after a central print change.

**Why ask:** Correlation can reveal a shared incident.

### C15-Q04: Who should see updates?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Branches need their jobs; head office needs a consolidated view.

**Why ask:** Scope notifications and access.

## Evidence in the fictional case

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** Customer hierarchy, device location and approval dependencies are not modelled consistently.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated move record conflicts with the asset sheet.
- Simulated tickets share a configuration-change timestamp.
- A simulated approval matrix separates branch and central authority.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Establish organization/site/device identities and separate access approval from spending approval; retain local child tickets under confirmed shared incidents.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Link related incidents for review; route approvals to scoped authorities; provide role-filtered views and readiness checks.

## Data fields

`organization_id`, `branch_id`, `asset_id`, `location_valid_from`, `parent_incident_id`, `spending_approver`, `access_contact`, `approval_status`, `appointment_status`

## Business rules

- C15-R01: Do not expose another branch’s restricted data.
- C15-R02: A planned appointment is not confirmed until required approvals and access exist.
- C15-R03: Parent incident closure does not prove every branch is restored.

## Acceptance tests

- C15-AT01: Similar branch names resolve by stable IDs.
- C15-AT02: Moving an asset preserves historical visits at the old site.
- C15-AT03: Restoring two branches leaves the third child ticket open.

## Human review

Organization hierarchy, approver limits and actual shared dependencies need confirmation.

## How to measure

**Primary metric:** Repeat trips caused by missing approval or incorrect location / branch service visits.
