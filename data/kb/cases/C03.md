# C03: Preventive maintenance without defined completion

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

11 staff; 38 school contracts with regular visits and reactive support.

“A visit usually means asking whether anything is broken and getting a signature. Different technicians check different things. We sometimes return for scheduled maintenance days after an emergency visit, yet a signed sheet can hide an inaccessible computer room.”

**Typical symptom:** A visit usually means asking whether anything is broken and getting a signature. We sometimes return for scheduled maintenance days after an emergency visit, yet a signed sheet can hide an inaccessible computer room.

## Current workflow

1. Administrator schedules school
2. Technician attends
3. Available equipment checked
4. Contact signs
5. Visit marked complete

## Diagnostic questions

### C03-Q01: Does the contract require a visit or named checks?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Several agreements list checks; others only specify frequency.

**Why ask:** Obligations must be interpreted per agreement.

**If the answer differs:** Where the agreement explicitly requires a distinct scheduled attendance, combining trips requires customer agreement even if checks overlap.

### C03-Q02: Did emergency work satisfy those checks?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** The technician fixed one printer but did not inspect the lab.

**Why ask:** Attendance cannot substitute for task evidence.

### C03-Q03: How are inaccessible rooms recorded?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** The whole visit is marked complete.

**Why ask:** Completion is too coarse.

### C03-Q04: Can visits be combined?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** The customer permits it if checks are done within the due period.

**Why ask:** Enables consolidation conditionally.

## Evidence in the fictional case

- C03-E02: A simulated report has a signature but no lab checklist.
- C03-E03: A simulated access note records a locked room.

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** The system tracks attendance instead of contract-level maintenance obligations and partial completion.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated contract extract lists printer inspection and workstation checks.
- A simulated report has a signature but no lab checklist.
- A simulated access note records a locked room.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Translate reviewed contracts into task lists; distinguish attended, partially completed and obligation fulfilled.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Track due tasks per site and asset; suggest combining eligible checks with reactive visits; carry incomplete work forward.

## Data fields

`contract_version`, `obligation_id`, `task_id`, `due_window`, `asset_scope`, `completed_at`, `evidence_ref`, `blocked_reason`, `acceptance_status`

## Business rules

- C03-R01: An emergency visit satisfies only evidenced tasks.
- C03-R02: Signatures confirm the stated scope, not inaccessible work.
- C03-R03: Blocked checks remain due unless an authorized exception is recorded.

## Acceptance tests

- C03-AT01: A printer repair does not close the lab obligation.
- C03-AT02: An inaccessible room creates a follow-up task.
- C03-AT03: A completed eligible checklist suppresses only the corresponding duplicate planned work.

## Human review

Ambiguous contract wording needs human interpretation before scheduling rules are encoded.

## How to measure

**Primary metric:** Evidenced obligations completed on time / obligations due; count attendance separately.
