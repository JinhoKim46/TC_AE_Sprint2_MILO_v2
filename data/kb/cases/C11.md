# C11: Contract revenue hides service burden

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

20 staff; approximately 100 recurring contracts.

“We know revenue and payroll but not service cost by account. Short remote calls look like long on-site repairs when both count as one ticket. Driving, repeat visits and van parts are missing. Renewal prices follow intuition.”

**Typical symptom:** We know revenue and payroll but not service cost by account. Renewal prices follow intuition.

## Current workflow

1. Serve customer
2. Record main task
3. Buy parts centrally
4. Receive recurring fee
5. Review revenue
6. Negotiate renewal

## Diagnostic questions

### C11-Q01: Are telephone and travel activities recorded?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Usually not.

**Why ask:** Ticket totals underestimate resource use.

**If the answer differs:** High service cost may reflect an intentional strategic account or temporary project; profitability ranking alone does not dictate termination.

### C11-Q02: Can parts be assigned to an account?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Large items yes; van consumables often no.

**Why ask:** Cost attribution has varying completeness.

### C11-Q03: Is technician time reliable?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Detailed reporters appear more expensive.

**Why ask:** Measurement bias can distort rankings.

### C11-Q04: What decision will the analysis support?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Scope and renewal discussions, not minute-by-minute employee monitoring.

**Why ask:** Choose proportionate data collection.

## Evidence in the fictional case

- C11-E01: A simulated activity sample contains calls missing from tickets.
- C11-E02: A simulated stock review finds unassigned part usage.
- C11-E03: A simulated contract comparison mixes remote and multi-person work.

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** Inconsistent activity and cost attribution prevents meaningful contract-level economics.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated activity sample contains calls missing from tickets.
- A simulated stock review finds unassigned part usage.
- A simulated contract comparison mixes remote and multi-person work.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Collect lightweight activity categories and parts attribution; disclose completeness; agree direct cost and overhead conventions separately.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Aggregate attributable time and material cost; show ranges or unknowns for missing inputs and flag expensive repeat patterns.

## Data fields

`contract_id`, `activity_type`, `staff_minutes`, `travel_minutes`, `material_cost`, `attribution_basis`, `cost_rate_version`, `revenue_period`, `completeness_flag`

## Business rules

- C11-R01: Do not equate ticket count with effort.
- C11-R02: Do not label contribution margin as net profit.
- C11-R03: Avoid counting shared travel twice.
- C11-R04: Suppress precise margin when material cost inputs are missing.

## Acceptance tests

- C11-AT01: A five-minute call and two-person visit receive different effort totals.
- C11-AT02: Missing rates result in unavailable cost, not zero cost.
- C11-AT03: Shared travel allocation reconciles to total travel.

## Human review

Loaded labor rates, overhead policy and actual usage must be measured locally.

## How to measure

**Primary metric:** Percentage of service effort attributable to a contract; only then report contribution using disclosed cost conventions.
