# C07: Repairs stall between diagnosis and parts arrival

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

Nine-person business PC and laptop repair provider.

“Technicians identify a part, office staff ask suppliers, and customers approve quotes. The gaps between those steps last days. An approval sent to a technician never reached purchasing. Shelves and spreadsheets disagree, and warranty return dates get missed.”

**Typical symptom:** Technicians identify a part, office staff ask suppliers, and customers approve quotes. Shelves and spreadsheets disagree, and warranty return dates get missed.

## Current workflow

1. Diagnose
2. Identify exact part
3. Request supplier quote
4. Seek customer approval
5. Order
6. Receive
7. Repair
8. Return loaner and close

## Diagnostic questions

### C07-Q01: What is waiting right now?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** The sheet says waiting for parts, but a supplier is actually waiting for a submodel.

**Why ask:** Status must expose the next dependency.

**If the answer differs:** A confirmed manufacturer backorder will not be shortened by reminders; communicate alternatives and expected dates instead.

### C07-Q02: Where does approval arrive?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Email and direct messages, sometimes only to the technician.

**Why ask:** Capture approval in the repair record.

### C07-Q03: Who checks overdue items?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Whoever receives a customer complaint.

**Why ask:** Follow-up ownership is absent.

### C07-Q04: Are return and loaner obligations tracked?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** They are handwritten on paper labels.

**Why ask:** Repair closure is not the only completion condition.

## Evidence in the fictional case

- C07-E01: A simulated approval message predates purchase by four days.
- C07-E02: A simulated supplier request lacks the full part number.
- C07-E03: A simulated return label has an approaching deadline.

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** Handoffs lack an accountable next action, complete part identity and explicit deadlines.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated approval message predates purchase by four days.
- A simulated supplier request lacks the full part number.
- A simulated return label has an approaching deadline.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Assign one repair owner; require exact part verification before purchase; separate waiting for information, approval, supplier and customer.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Track dependency states, deadline reminders and linked loan/return obligations; prepare customer updates from verified status.

## Data fields

`repair_id`, `serial`, `submodel`, `part_number`, `next_action`, `action_owner`, `due_at`, `approval_ref`, `order_ref`, `supplier_eta`, `return_deadline`, `loaner_id`

## Business rules

- C07-R01: No purchase without applicable approval and verified part identity.
- C07-R02: A supplier ETA is not a confirmed appointment.
- C07-R03: Closing repair does not erase an open loaner or return obligation.

## Acceptance tests

- C07-AT01: Direct-message approval becomes a reviewable linked record.
- C07-AT02: Overdue supplier clarification alerts the responsible person.
- C07-AT03: A repaired laptop with an outstanding loaner retains that obligation.
