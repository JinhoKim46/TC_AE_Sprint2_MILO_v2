# C18: Warehouse, van and customer stock diverge

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

24 staff; 16 field technicians, central warehouse and customer-held loan equipment.

“Purchases, warehouse counts and van notes never agree. We buy parts that another technician already has. Faulty items return without job references, losing warranty credits. Cheap cables should not require the same administration as expensive serialized components.”

**Typical symptom:** Purchases, warehouse counts and van notes never agree. Cheap cables should not require the same administration as expensive serialized components.

## Current workflow

1. Purchase
2. Receive
3. Transfer to van
4. Install or loan
5. Return faulty item
6. Seek credit or scrap
7. Reconcile sporadically

## Diagnostic questions

### C18-Q01: Which items need individual tracking?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Loaners and high-value components; cheap cables can use bin counts.

**Why ask:** Match control cost to risk.

**If the answer differs:** For low-cost bulk consumables, detailed serial tracking would add waste; use replenishment bins and periodic counts.

### C18-Q02: Are faulty and usable parts separated?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Not consistently.

**Why ask:** Inventory disposition matters.

### C18-Q03: Can a return be traced to purchase?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Often no job or purchase reference survives.

**Why ask:** Warranty recovery needs provenance.

### C18-Q04: Are transfers acknowledged?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Staff send messages but do not confirm receipt.

**Why ask:** Movement needs both endpoints.

## Evidence in the fictional case

- C18-E01: A simulated count finds stock in a van that purchasing considered unavailable.
- C18-E02: A simulated faulty return has no purchase reference.
- C18-E03: A simulated loaner remains at a customer after job closure.

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** Inventory lacks a consistent movement ledger, disposition and serialized-loan lifecycle.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated count finds stock in a van that purchasing considered unavailable.
- A simulated faulty return has no purchase reference.
- A simulated loaner remains at a customer after job closure.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Segment stock by tracking needs; log high-value transfers and returns; quarantine faulty items and assign return owners.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Use barcode-assisted movements, location balances, loan obligations and warranty deadline tracking.

## Data fields

`item_id`, `sku`, `serial_nullable`, `tracking_class`, `from_location`, `to_location`, `disposition`, `movement_id`, `job_ref`, `purchase_ref`, `loan_due`, `warranty_case`

## Business rules

- C18-R01: Quarantined stock is not available for assignment.
- C18-R02: Serialized items have one current custody location.
- C18-R03: Movement retries are idempotent.
- C18-R04: Scrap and write-off require the designated authority.

## Acceptance tests

- C18-AT01: A serialized loaner cannot be issued to two customers.
- C18-AT02: A faulty return increases quarantine, not usable stock.
- C18-AT03: Repeated scan submission creates one transfer.

## Human review

Tracking thresholds, warranty eligibility and opening balances need approval and physical checks.

## How to measure

**Primary metric:** Value-weighted stock accuracy and recoverable warranty credits collected; track entry time per movement.
