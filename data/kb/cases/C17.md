# C17: Vendor escalation becomes a ticket graveyard

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

Ten-person office network, Wi-Fi and general IT service provider.

“The internet provider wants wired tests; the application vendor wants logs. Our notes rarely say where or when tests occurred. Second technicians repeat them. An access point replacement did not fix a meeting-room problem that returned during busy events. Waiting for vendor can last indefinitely.”

**Typical symptom:** The internet provider wants wired tests; the application vendor wants logs. Waiting for vendor can last indefinitely.

## Current workflow

1. Customer reports slowness
2. Technician tests locally
3. Suspected supplier contacted
4. More evidence requested
5. Customer waits
6. Workaround applied
7. Ticket closed or forgotten

## Diagnostic questions

### C17-Q01: Which conditions reproduce the issue?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Busy meetings, but tests were done in an empty room.

**Why ask:** Test context matters.

**If the answer differs:** A supplier may have a real unresolved external defect; the service firm can manage evidence and communications without claiming to fix it.

### C17-Q02: Are results timestamped and tied to a device?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Usually not.

**Why ask:** Prevent ambiguous or duplicated evidence.

### C17-Q03: Who follows up with the vendor?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** No named owner after escalation.

**Why ask:** Waiting still requires ownership.

### C17-Q04: Was the workaround verified under original conditions?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** No.

**Why ask:** Restoration is not causal resolution.

## Evidence in the fictional case

- C17-E01: A simulated test log omits load conditions.
- C17-E02: A simulated vendor request remains unanswered.
- C17-E03: A simulated closure note records only temporary restoration.

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** Evidence lacks reproducible context and escalations lose accountable ownership.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated test log omits load conditions.
- A simulated vendor request remains unanswered.
- A simulated closure note records only temporary restoration.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Record test context, results and next vendor action; separate restored, monitoring and resolved states.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Build structured investigation timelines and vendor follow-up tasks; draft evidence packages with sensitive information removed.

## Data fields

`incident_id`, `test_id`, `timestamp`, `endpoint`, `connection_type`, `load_context`, `observation`, `vendor_case_id`, `followup_owner`, `due_at`, `workaround`, `verification_plan`

## Business rules

- C17-R01: A workaround cannot automatically close the underlying investigation.
- C17-R02: Every waiting state has an owner and review date.
- C17-R03: Vendor evidence exports exclude credentials and unrelated customer data.

## Acceptance tests

- C17-AT01: A second technician sees prior conditions and results.
- C17-AT02: Overdue vendor follow-up escalates internally.
- C17-AT03: Recurrence under original load prevents confirmed-resolution status.

## Human review

Supplier support windows and permissible diagnostic data vary by agreement.

## How to measure

**Primary metric:** Age of externally blocked incidents and repeated diagnostic effort; track recurrence after closure.
