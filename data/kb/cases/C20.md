# C20: Owner memory is the operating system

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

Six staff: owner, four technicians and administrator; plans to hire two more technicians.

“I coordinate everything through messages and memory. When I took three days away, someone revisited a customer before the part arrived, and invoices stopped because nobody knew what I promised. We have spreadsheets and calendars, but I connect them in my head.”

**Typical symptom:** I coordinate everything through messages and memory. We have spreadsheets and calendars, but I connect them in my head.

## Current workflow

1. Owner reviews messages
2. Owner directs staff
3. Staff ask owner for decisions
4. Owner resolves exceptions
5. Owner tells office what to bill

## Diagnostic questions

### C20-Q01: Which commitments exist only in your head?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Call-before-visit rules, parts approvals and invoice holds.

**Why ask:** Capture operational exceptions first.

**If the answer differs:** Some decisions legitimately require the owner’s commercial judgement; do not automate every escalation or assume full delegation is desired.

### C20-Q02: Which decisions can staff safely make?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Routine scheduling and approved parts use, but spending limits are unwritten.

**Why ask:** Delegation needs boundaries.

### C20-Q03: Why did the premature visit happen?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Nobody could see the pending part dependency.

**Why ask:** Missing next-action visibility, not technician competence.

### C20-Q04: How much implementation time is realistic?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Short daily reviews; no lengthy parallel data entry.

**Why ask:** Start small and retire duplication.

## Evidence in the fictional case

- C20-E01: A simulated message contains a parts promise absent from the calendar.
- C20-E02: A simulated invoice has an owner-only hold condition.
- C20-E03: A simulated absence review identifies repeated routine questions.

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** Commitments, dependencies and delegated authority are person-bound rather than recorded in a shared workflow.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated message contains a parts promise absent from the calendar.
- A simulated invoice has an owner-only hold condition.
- A simulated absence review identifies repeated routine questions.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Create one lightweight commitment board with owner, next action and blocker; publish agreed routine decision boundaries.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Capture message-derived task drafts for confirmation; remind responsible staff about dependencies; escalate exceptions beyond agreed authority.

## Data fields

`commitment_id`, `customer_id`, `source_ref`, `promise`, `responsible_person`, `next_action`, `blocker`, `due_at`, `delegated_limit`, `invoice_hold`, `release_condition`

## Business rules

- C20-R01: Extracted promises remain drafts until confirmed.
- C20-R02: Invoice holds require an explicit release decision.
- C20-R03: A missing part blocks a visit that requires it.
- C20-R04: Decisions beyond authority escalate instead of guessing.

## Acceptance tests

- C20-AT01: Pending part prevents a misleading ready-to-visit state.
- C20-AT02: Staff can handle an approved routine decision during owner absence.
- C20-AT03: A complaint hold survives unrelated job completion.

## Human review

Actual delegation limits and which promises are binding require the owner’s review.

## How to measure

**Primary metric:** Owner interruptions for routine decisions per staffed day; track overdue commitments and incorrect delegated decisions.
