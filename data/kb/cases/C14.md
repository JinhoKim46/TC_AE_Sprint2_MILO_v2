# C14: Renewal dates and terms diverge

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

Rental and maintenance provider; approximately 140 active agreements.

“Contracts, billing and the renewal spreadsheet are separate. Email extensions and added equipment remain in messages. A customer arranged a competitor’s copier while we thought renewal was months away. Sales lacks a reliable service history before discussing the next agreement.”

**Typical symptom:** Contracts, billing and the renewal spreadsheet are separate. Sales lacks a reliable service history before discussing the next agreement.

## Current workflow

1. Sales agrees terms
2. Signed file stored
3. Billing configured
4. Changes exchanged by email
5. Renewal sheet updated selectively
6. Customer contacted late

## Diagnostic questions

### C14-Q01: Which document controls the date?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** A later email extension was accepted but never reflected in the sheet.

**Why ask:** Need amendment provenance.

**If the answer differs:** A customer with a deliberate competitive tender needs tender tracking; an earlier reminder alone may not retain it.

### C14-Q02: Who owns renewal follow-up?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Sales and accounts each assumed the other was tracking it.

**Why ask:** Ownership is explicit work.

### C14-Q03: Are notice deadlines different from expiry?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Some contracts require earlier action.

**Why ask:** Expiry alone is not enough.

### C14-Q04: Can offers be sent automatically?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Service complaints or old equipment can change the proposal.

**Why ask:** Prepare review, not generic sales sending.

## Evidence in the fictional case

- C14-E01: A simulated amendment changes the end date.
- C14-E02: A simulated spreadsheet retains the original date.
- C14-E03: A simulated account has unresolved repeat failures.

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** Agreement versions and decision deadlines are not reconciled into an owned renewal workflow.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated amendment changes the end date.
- A simulated spreadsheet retains the original date.
- A simulated account has unresolved repeat failures.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Review the current agreement and amendments; assign an account owner and separate notice, review and end dates.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Extract date candidates for review; maintain effective terms; prepare account summaries and internal renewal tasks.

## Data fields

`agreement_id`, `version`, `amendment_ref`, `effective_date`, `end_date`, `notice_deadline`, `account_owner`, `service_summary`, `equipment_condition`, `renewal_stage`

## Business rules

- C14-R01: Conflicting dates require review before reminders claim certainty.
- C14-R02: No automatic renewal offer without account-owner approval.
- C14-R03: A new amendment supersedes applicable fields while preserving history.

## Acceptance tests

- C14-AT01: Conflicting expiry dates create a review item.
- C14-AT02: Notice deadline triggers earlier than expiry when configured.
- C14-AT03: Open service complaints appear in the review brief.

## Human review

Notice interpretations, negotiation lead time and completeness of amendments need review.

## How to measure

**Primary metric:** Agreements reviewed before their applicable decision deadline / agreements with deadlines in period.
