# C09: Installation readiness discovered on site

> Synthetic teaching case: invented for learning, not a customer record. Example answers and evidence belong to this fictional business only; ask the owner rather than assume they apply.

## Business and opening narrative

17 staff; school maintenance plus holiday computer-room projects.

“Sales surveys are scattered across photos and messages. On installation day we discover missing ports, unexpected software or data migration expectations. Maintenance emergencies pull technicians away from projects during the busiest school-holiday weeks.”

**Typical symptom:** Sales surveys are scattered across photos and messages. Maintenance emergencies pull technicians away from projects during the busiest school-holiday weeks.

## Current workflow

1. Sales survey

## Diagnostic questions

### C09-Q01: Who signs off the deployment configuration?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Sales and the school discussed it, but no version was approved.

**Why ask:** Rework originates before imaging.

**If the answer differs:** A late customer change after a documented sign-off is change control, not necessarily an initial survey failure.

### C09-Q02: Are access and ports verified?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Photos do not establish working network capacity.

**Why ask:** A survey needs functional checks.

### C09-Q03: Is migration included?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Teachers assumed it, while the quote was silent.

**Why ask:** Surface scope ambiguity before commitment.

### C09-Q04: Is maintenance capacity reserved?

**Example answer in this fictional case:** Project and service calendars are separate.

**Why ask:** Capacity conflict spans both work types.

## Diagnosis

**Supported in this case:** Project release is based on a calendar date rather than verified scope, site readiness and resource capacity.

## Minimum evidence before reusing this pattern

- A simulated survey lacks port test results.
- A simulated quote does not mention migration.
- A simulated project plan allocates staff already on reactive coverage.

## Interventions

**Process first:** Introduce a named readiness owner and configuration sign-off; reserve service capacity and use explicit scope-change decisions.

**Automation and bounded AI help:** Track prerequisites, dependencies and approved configuration versions; block release when essential readiness evidence is missing.

## Data fields

`project_id`, `room_id`, `port_test`, `access_confirmed`, `configuration_version`, `software_license_status`, `migration_scope`, `signoff_ref`, `equipment_received`, `resource_reservation`

## Business rules

- C09-R01: Do not embed credentials in readiness documents.
- C09-R02: A post-signoff configuration change triggers impact review.
- C09-R03: A blocked project cannot be silently labeled ready to meet a date.

## Acceptance tests

- C09-AT01: Missing network test blocks deployment release.
- C09-AT02: A revised image invalidates outdated configuration approval.
- C09-AT03: Resource conflicts appear before appointments are promised.

## Human review

Customer data handling and software licensing decisions need explicit approval.

## How to measure

**Primary metric:** Installation hours spent on unplanned rework / installation hours; track readiness pass rate and reactive service backlog.
