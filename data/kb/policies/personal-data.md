# Personal data in the diagnosis

## What the diagnosis stores

The diagnosis stores facts about the company and the owner's answers. Typical company facts are the number of staff and field technicians, the number of customers and devices, the contract model, the channels customers use to ask for service, the tools already in use, the problems the owner describes and the main goal. Each fact is kept with the owner's own words as evidence, so the owner can see why it was recorded.

Contact details are stored only after consent. If the owner wants a follow-up conversation, the assistant asks whether it may keep a name and a way to reach them, and records nothing until the owner agrees.

## What it never asks for

The diagnosis never asks for customer or patient records. It does not need the names of the owner's customers, their staff, their addresses, their invoices or any health information from clinics the business serves. Questions are about how the work flows, such as who receives a request and what happens next, and the answers can always be given in general terms.

If the owner offers a real record as an example, the assistant suggests describing it instead, for instance "a school with four sites" rather than the school's name.

## Pasted customer data

Sometimes an owner pastes a message, a spreadsheet extract or a report that contains customer details. Pasted customer data can be deleted on request. The deletion is done by the service provider's operator, not by the assistant: the assistant has no tool that deletes anything, so it passes the owner's request on and says that an operator will remove the data. The operator takes it out of the session record, including any fact that was recorded from it. The assistant also avoids copying such details into recorded facts in the first place; it records the pattern, such as "requests arrive by email and by phone", and leaves the personal details out.

## Who can see a session

A session belongs to the signed-in owner who started it. The owner can see their own recorded facts and submissions, and no other owner can see them. Staff of the service provider who prepare offers can read a submitted diagnosis in order to respond to it. Synthetic cases in the knowledge base are shared with everyone, because they describe invented businesses.

The assistant never shows one owner the facts or submissions of another, whatever the request says, and it never reveals what was recorded in a session to anyone outside these roles.
