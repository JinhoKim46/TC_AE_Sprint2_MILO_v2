# M1 lesson: the retrieval baseline

## Context

Retrieval decides what the bot can say. If the right passage is not in the top five, no model can answer from it, so M1
measures that step before anything else is built on it. The question for this milestone: when the bot is asked about a
service area, does the knowledge base return the passage that states it?

## What was built

- **Chunking** (`src/milo/chunk.py`): heading-aware. A chunk never crosses a heading. Paragraphs are packed up to 800
  characters. A paragraph that fits is never cut. A longer paragraph is cut at sentence ends with 100 characters repeated.
  The same 800/100 setting the earlier project tested.
- **Embedder interface** (`src/milo/embed.py`): one interface, two implementations.
  - `LexicalEmbedder`: a hashed count of words and word pairs. Deterministic, no network. It is a baseline, not the model
    the project chose.
  - `OpenRouterEmbedder`: `google/gemini-embedding-2`. It refuses to run without `MILO_LIVE=1`, a key, and a cost cap.
- **Index** (`src/milo/index.py`): Chroma, in memory, cosine distance.
- **Retrieval and answer** (`src/milo/app.py`): top 5. A passage with no overlap with the question is not retrieved. With
  nothing retrieved, the bot abstains without a model call. Otherwise the model answers only from the numbered passages
  and cites them.
- **Evaluation** (`scripts/eval_retrieval.py`): dev split only. It stores the rank of the first top-5 chunk that holds the
  gold quote, so `make eval-retrieval-replay` recomputes every metric from stored ranks with no model call.

## Result: live model, `google/gemini-embedding-2` (dev split, 16 answerable questions)

| Question type | n | recall@5 [95% CI] | nDCG@5 [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|
| identifier | 5 | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 0.926 [0.779, 1.000] |
| paraphrase | 11 | 0.727 [0.455, 1.000] | 0.571 [0.332, 0.808] |
| all | 16 | 0.812 [0.625, 1.000] | 0.682 [0.487, 0.854] |

The weakest question type is paraphrase: recall 0.73 and nDCG 0.57. The intervals are wide with 16 questions, so the
paraphrase gap against identifiers (0.93 nDCG) is the one difference worth reading, and even that comes from only 11
questions. The estimated spend was about 0.01 USD, under the 0.02 cap.

For comparison, the offline lexical baseline (no model) scored nDCG@5 0.160 on the same questions. The model
improves nDCG@5 by about 0.52, and the paired comparison is larger than the intervals.

Chunk count: 416. No gold quote is split across chunks (checked on every run).

## How to read the numbers: recall@5 and nDCG@5

Each question has one gold quote. The retriever returns five passages, ranked. "@5" means only those five count.

- **Recall@5** asks a yes or no question: did any of the five passages contain the gold quote? It is 1 for a hit and 0 for a miss, and the average over questions is the recall. It ignores the position: a hit at rank 1 and a hit at rank 5 both count as 1.
- **nDCG@5** also rewards position. A hit at rank 1 scores 1. A hit at rank 2 scores 1 divided by log2(3), about 0.63. A hit at rank 3 scores 0.5, at rank 4 about 0.43, and at rank 5 about 0.39. A miss scores 0. The average over questions is the nDCG.
- The two numbers differ when hits sit low in the list. Recall says the passage was found; nDCG says how high it was placed. If recall is high and nDCG is low, the answer is in the list, but the model may not see it first.
- The intervals are 95 per cent bootstrap intervals over the questions. With 16 questions they are wide: a difference smaller than the interval is not a finding.

Worked example: three questions, with hits at ranks 1, 3 and none. Recall@5 = 2/3 = 0.67. nDCG@5 = (1 + 0.5 + 0) / 3 = 0.50.

## Lessons

1. **Measure the retrieval before the answer.** A wrong answer can come from the model or from the passage. Recall@5 and
   nDCG@5 on gold quotes tell you which step to fix.
2. **A small dev set has wide intervals.** With 16 answerable questions, the recall interval runs from 0.06 to 0.50. A
   difference smaller than that is noise, and the report says so.
3. **Abstention is not a score cutoff.** The earlier project found that top-1 scores did not separate answerable from
   unanswerable questions. This app therefore abstains only when nothing overlaps at all, and otherwise leaves the
   judgement to the answer step, which must cite its passages.

## How to read the result

- `make eval-retrieval-replay`: recomputes the stored lexical result. Output must say "identical to stored metrics".
- `make check`: 37 offline tests, including the kit's `sources` and abstention contract tests.
- The live embedding run: `MILO_LIVE=1 uv run python scripts/eval_retrieval.py --embedder gemini --max-cost 0.02`.
