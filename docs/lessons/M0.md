# M0 lesson: domain and data

## Context

A RAG chatbot can only be as good as two datasets: the **knowledge base** it retrieves from, and the **gold set**
that says what a correct retrieval is. Every later number (recall, nDCG, faithfulness) is measured against these, so
their quality decides whether later improvements are real. M0 builds both, plus the tool cases and attacks that M3
and M4 measure against.

The 7 modules group the package's 20 blueprints: request-intake, dispatch-planning, contract-management,
stock-control, meter-billing, service-reports, work-tracking.

## Three data-quality lessons from this milestone

1. **JSON is not prose.** The package's retrieval records hold raw JSON (`{"context": "...", "narrative": ...}`).
   An embedding model sees quotes, braces and key names as text. The converter renders the same content as
   headings and sentences.
2. **Repeated text makes documents look alike.** About a third of the package's sentence slots repeat across ten or
   more records ("No price assigned..."). Repeated sentences pull unrelated cases together in embedding space. The
   converter moves any sentence that appears in four or more case families into one method-notes article, and a test
   (`test_no_sentence_repeats_across_many_articles`) keeps it that way.
3. **Duplicates break the gold set.** The package's playbook and blueprint for a case repeat about 61% of the case's
   sentences. A gold quote would then exist in three documents, and a retriever that found the "wrong" copy would
   be scored as a miss. Each case family is therefore one article; every gold quote now appears in exactly one
   article.

A review then caught the fourth lesson: **a test case with two defensible answers measures the grader, not the
model.** Two documents answered the same gold question (a price in a solution article and in the pricing table), so
filters now pin the intended document; the policy said "the assistant asks for approval" while the tool case
expected an immediate call, so the policy now says the application pauses the call; and "what would it cost" cases
now state the recorded facts, so asking a question back is no longer an equally right answer.

The package's own 120 records stay unchanged in `data/source/`: in M1 they are a comparison arm, so you will see
these three effects as numbers, not just as arguments.

## How to read the result

- `python3 scripts/check_project_data.py data` (the kit's checker): `OK`. It verifies every gold quote is verbatim in
  its document and the minimum counts.
- `make check`: 24 tests. Beyond the kit's checks, they enforce: articles reproducible from the package; no JSON
  syntax and no MILO v1 names in articles; no long sentence in four or more articles; no brand names or emails;
  evaluation material (the package's records and 30 scenarios) never inside `data/kb`; every module priced and its
  article stating the rate card's figure; paraphrase questions share at most 30% of words with their quote;
  unanswerable questions absent from the knowledge base; tool cases use only declared tools and arguments.
- One incident worth knowing: a repo-wide `ruff format` silently rewrote two of the package's scripts. The integrity
  test now catches that, and `data/source` is excluded from formatting.
